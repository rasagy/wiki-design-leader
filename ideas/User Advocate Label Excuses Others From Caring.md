Declaring yourself the "user advocate" sounds noble but is organizationally counterproductive — it implicitly signals that everyone else is excused from caring about users, and positions design as a defensive watchdog rather than a collaborative driver. A stronger posture is to make user-centricity everyone's shared responsibility, not a design-owned title.
