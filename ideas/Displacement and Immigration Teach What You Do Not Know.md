Rafael Viñoly describes immigration as "probably the only university that can teach you how much you don't know" — the experience of being radically out of context strips away assumptions and produces a kind of epistemic humility that formal education rarely achieves. Leaders who have been genuinely dislocated — culturally, professionally, or personally — often carry a curiosity and adaptability that compounds over a career.
