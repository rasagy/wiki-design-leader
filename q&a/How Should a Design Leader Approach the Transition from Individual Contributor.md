question: "How Should a Design Leader Approach the Transition from Individual Contributor"

## Answer

The transition from individual contributor to design leader is not an evolution of craft — it is a change of medium, from making design to creating the conditions in which others make great design. The key is committing fully despite being incompletely prepared, and deliberately building skills that design training never covered.

This question touches on **4 areas**:

### Accepting the Identity Shift

The hardest part of the transition is letting go of the work that earned you the role. Your output is no longer pixels or prototypes — it is the environment, structure, and people around you.

- [[ideas/Leadership Transition Requires an Entirely New Skillset]]
- [[ideas/Design Leader Conducts the Orchestra Not Plays]]
- [[ideas/Your Team Is Your Primary Customer as a Leader]]
- [[ideas/Design Leaders Can Stay Close to Craft and Strategy]]

### Committing Despite Incomplete Readiness

Waiting to feel fully prepared is a trap. The imposter feeling is nearly universal — even among leaders at successful firms — and reflects awareness of the role's demands, not actual incompetence.

- [[ideas/Committed Not Prepared Mindset for Leadership]]
- [[ideas/Most Design Leaders Feel Like They Are Making It Up]]
- [[ideas/Leaders Who Fail Frequently and Learn Outperform]]
- [[ideas/Vulnerability and Admitting Confusion Builds Leader Trust]]

### Deliberately Building People Skills

Design training builds craft, not the interpersonal skills leadership demands. Negotiation, conflict resolution, and giving feedback must be actively sought out — they won't develop on their own.

- [[ideas/Soft Skills Are Perennially Underdeveloped in Design Leaders]]
- [[ideas/Management as a Form of Caring for People]]
- [[ideas/One-on-Ones Are Not Status Updates]]

### Reframing How You Communicate and Reason

Designers rely on intuition; leaders must translate that intuition into business language. This isn't about abandoning design thinking — it's about making it legible to people who don't share your vocabulary.

- [[ideas/Designers Must Reframe Intuition as Business Logic]]

> [!note] From notes
> *Design Leadership Handbook* describes Andy Law (Netflix) as a model for this transition: he researched the role before accepting it, studied failures as much as successes, and entered with the mindset "I'll never be 100% prepared, but I'll always be 100% committed." *The Making of a Manager* (Zhuo) adds that the imposter feeling is nearly universal among first-time managers — it signals awareness of the gap, not incompetence. [[notes/Design Leadership Handbook - Aarron Walter and Eli Woolery (2019)]] [[notes/The Making of a Manager - What to Do When Everyone Looks to - Zhuo, Julie (2019)]]
